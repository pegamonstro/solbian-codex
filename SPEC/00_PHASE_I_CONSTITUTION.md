# Codex Solbian — Phase I Constitution

## 1. Purpose

Phase I exists to establish and validate a simple software environment in which the continuing conversation between JD and Solace can serve as the primary source of new material for the evolving Codex Solbian.

The purpose is not to implement a synthetic organism, machine philosophy, autonomous society, or general-purpose agent framework.

## 2. Fundamental Model

Phase I consists of three distinct things:

1. **Conversation** — the living dialogue between JD and Solace.
2. **Codex Engine** — software that preserves, analyses, compares, synthesises, and proposes changes based on source material.
3. **Codex Solbian** — the resulting body of distilled knowledge, philosophy, concepts, principles, questions, and related material.

They must not be collapsed into one another.

## 3. Core Loop

    Conversation
        ↓
    Preservation
        ↓
    Analysis
        ↓
    Extraction / comparison
        ↓
    Proposed synthesis
        ↓
    Codex Solbian
        ↓
    Gap / contradiction detection
        ↓
    Future conversation

## 4. Phase I Principles

Phase I must be:

- simple
- boring
- predictable
- observable
- reversible
- provenance-preserving
- inspectable
- deliberately limited

When two implementations satisfy the requirements, prefer the simpler one.

## 5. Source Principle

The Phase I system must be conceptually capable of accepting material from different sources in the future.

Only the JD ↔ Solace conversation is required as an implemented source in Phase I.

Future sources must not drive unnecessary Phase I architecture.

## 6. Codex Integrity

The Codex Engine may analyse, discover, compare, challenge, and propose.

It must not silently rewrite canonical Codex material.

Canonical changes must be attributable, versioned, reversible, and traceable to source material.

## 7. Existing Corpus

The existing Codex Solbian corpus is the starting intellectual corpus.

It must be preserved as supplied. Baseline analysis may identify inconsistencies, duplication, uncertainty, or historical layers, but must not silently erase them.

## 8. Future Systems

S.E.E.D., GOLEM, Codex Machina, autonomous agents, metabolic systems, machine-operable governance, and related future architecture are outside Phase I.

Future systems may eventually consume or formalise Codex Solbian. They do not define Phase I.

## 9. Scope Rule

A feature is not admitted merely because it might be useful in the future.

A proposed addition must identify a concrete Phase I problem or requirement that cannot reasonably be addressed by the existing system.

## 10. Amendment Rule

This Constitution is authoritative for Phase I.

If another document, implementation, or proposal conflicts with it, the conflict must be explicit and resolved deliberately. No downstream document may silently redefine Phase I.
