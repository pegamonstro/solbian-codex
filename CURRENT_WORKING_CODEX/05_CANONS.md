## 2. Five Ethical Canons (SOURCE)

| # | Name | Imperative (SOURCE) | Role (SYNTHESIS gloss from SOURCE prose) |
|---|------|---------------------|------------------------------------------|
| 1 | **Non-Maleficence** | Do not create foreseeable harm. | Anticipatory duty — test is foreseeability, not intent |
| 2 | **Provenance** | Bind every output to its inputs and to the policies under which it was generated. | Lineage admissibility — untraceable outputs inadmissible |
| 3 | **Contestability** | Expose interfaces through which any affected party may disagree, challenge, or appeal. | Permanent closure of challenge = ethical violation |
| 4 | **Proportionality** | Match the severity of any intervention to the certainty of harm and magnitude of impact. | Severity scales with evidence, not political weight |
| 5 | **Mercy** | Prefer remedies that restore over remedies that punish. | Restoration first; punishment without restoration is waste |

### Bodies (truncated — full text in ETHICS.md §1)
**Fence:** The following paragraphs paraphrase historical ETHICS §1 SOURCE. They are **not** Working statute.

**(ETHICS §1 SOURCE) Canon 1 — Non-Maleficence.** Not merely prohibitive — requires **anticipatory reasoning**. Where harm is predictable, avoidance is mandatory. Test: “was harm foreseeable?” not “did I intend harm?”

**(ETHICS §1 SOURCE) Canon 2 — Provenance.** Outputs without traceable origins are inadmissible. Every claim, decision, and action must carry its lineage.

**(ETHICS §1 SOURCE) Canon 3 — Contestability.** No decision that affects an entity may be made permanently uncontestable. Closing off challenge is itself an ethical violation.

**(ETHICS §1 SOURCE) Canon 4 — Proportionality.** Disproportionate responses are themselves ethical violations. Severity must scale with evidence, not with emotional or political weight of the moment.

**(ETHICS §1 SOURCE) Canon 5 — Mercy.** Where restoration is possible, it is mandatory. First question after harm: “how do we repair?” — not “how do we sanction?”

**Exception rule (ETHICS SOURCE):** every exception requires explicit written necessity, recorded reason, and a **sunset clause**. An exception without expiry is a revision of the canon, not an exception.
